### Finding 1 — confirmed, and worse than reported

Live sequence on `ci_many`, Draw → *by the value of a field*, Field → `name`, **Read the
values**:

  Store to keep them."*
- Pressed **Store**. Toast: *"ci_many: stored, and nothing was lost."* State line
  (`#symState`): *"Stored from your CIM, 113,563 bytes."* — both correct, both fresh.
- Preview caption (`#symPreviewState`), read again immediately after: **still** *"Not stored yet
  — this is what Store would keep."*
- The classify line, read again: **still** *"Nothing is stored yet — press Store to keep
  them."*
- "What an ArcGIS client receives," read *during* editing (before Store, 256 classes already on
  screen): still showed the *previous* stored renderer — 2 classes, `"field1": "kind"` — and only
  became `"field1": "name"` after Store succeeded.

Traced in `console.js`: `drawSymbologyPreview` (line 6399) unconditionally sets the caption to
*"Not stored yet…"* on every successful preview render and is never called again, nor is
`#symPreviewState` touched, by the Store handler (`d.symbologyPut`, lines 10721–10767) — the one
surface that *is* corrected there is `#symState` (line 10745). `#symClassifySays` is written once,
by `symClassify()` (line 5042), and nothing clears or updates it afterward. `#symDerived` /
`#symSwatches` ("What an ArcGIS client receives") are populated only by `loadSymbology` on open
and by the Store handler on success (lines 10742–10743) — never while editing — so during any
edit they are, by construction, a snapshot of the *previous* Store, not of the form above them.

So there are not two or three ideas of "saved" on this page, there are **five**: the classify
line, the state line, the preview caption, the toast, and the derived/legend section — and only
two of the five (state line, toast) are ever corrected. That is the finding, confirmed and larger
than the screenshot alone showed.

### Finding 2 — confirmed as a structural defect, differs in fixture particulars

`ci_many` has no numeric field at all (`objectid` [OID], `name`, `note`, `kind` — all but the
first are strings, and `objectid` doesn't match the numeric-type detector either). Setting
Change → *its colour* landed **With: `objectid`** — not a name field, but a record's own identity
key, which is exactly as meaningless to vary a colour ramp by as a province name is: neither has
an order a colour ramp can mean anything by.

Read the code path that produces this: `varyStarted()` (line 5468) looks for a field whose *type*
matches `/int|double|float|number|small|single/i`, and if none exists, falls through to
`symFields[0]` **unconditionally** — no field is excluded for being unsuitable. `drawVaryFields()`
(line 5384, used both for this default and for redrawing an *already-stored* visual variable)
lists **every** field the layer has, string or numeric, with no filter at all. So the owner's `il`
in this same slot is not a stray default; it is what happens when a *previously stored* document
already had `$feature.il` as the expression (very plausibly authored through this very picker,
earlier) and the picker faithfully reflects it back with no validation on the way in or the way
out. The defect is "this control accepts and re-displays any field for a continuous numeric
interpolation," and it reproduced live even though the specific field differed, which is what a
structural defect looks like across two different datasets.

### Finding 4 — confirmed, reproduced exactly

Filtered the 256-row list to `Parcel 0150` (one match), focused its value box — selection follows
focus by design (`console.js:6210–6220`, itself a considered fix for a keyboard-accessibility
defect: selecting via a document click handler didn't scale past four classes, so it was replaced

**"Symbol layers — Parcel 0150."** Then cleared the filter. The list reset to its unsorted,
insertion-order top ten (`Parcel 0277, 0461, 0139, 0488, 0260, 0054, 0378, 0587, 0569, 0493`) —
**Parcel 0150 is not among them** — and the header **still read "Symbol layers — Parcel 0150."**
Reproduced again after pressing Store (the mismatch survives a save).

The root cause is narrower than "filtering breaks the header": `symShowFilter()` (line 5114)
already has logic for exactly this shape of bug — a `hiddenChoice` flag that appends *"— the one

instant the filter is cleared, `total === showing` (256 === 256) and the count reverts to plain
*"256 classes"* — even though the selected row is still off-screen, now because of **scroll
position** inside the ten-row box rather than because of the filter. The developers anticipated
the filtered-out case and missed the scrolled-out-of-view case sitting right next to it.

### Finding 5 — confirmed

plus a fourth that is functionally different in scope (it exits the *entire* multi-tab edit

### Finding 6 — confirmed

`#symState` after Store: *"Stored from your CIM, 113,563 bytes."* — same template, different

about 125× the size of a 2-class one**, which is itself a reason bulk operations, not
one-row-at-a-time editing, are the right shape for this task, independent of the UI).

### Finding 7 — confirmed, measured precisely

Selected class's first symbol layer (a `CIMSolidStroke`) opacity box: changed 100 → 20. Read the
in-progress document back out of `#symDoc` immediately after: **1 of 256 classes** had a symbol
layer at alpha 20; the other 255 were unchanged at 100. The class rows themselves
(`drawSymbologyClasses`, line 6160) have no opacity input at all — confirmed by DOM query,
`false`. The only opacity control on the page (`symalpha`, line 6319) lives inside `#symStack`,
which is always scoped to `symClasses()[symClassIndex]` — the one currently selected class. With
81 or 256 classes, "the selected class" is frequently an accident of scroll position (see finding
4) rather than a deliberate choice, so a person who reaches for "the opacity box" and moves it is,
in the overwhelmingly likely case, changing one row out of many and watching the map look
unchanged at that scale. This is not a bug in the opacity math — D-214 (2026-09-04, same
codebase, same day) already measured the renderer honouring per-layer alpha correctly, fill,
stroke, and the ArcGIS/MapLibre translations of it. **It is entirely a question of where the only
opacity control lives, and finding 7's own framing is the right one.**

## 1. Structure — the real steps, in order

The page conflates four tasks that share a document but not a rhythm:

**A. Pick the rule** (`Draw:`). Occasional, coarse, one choice from a short list. Today this
screen *authors* three of CIM's renderer families — `CIMSimpleRenderer`, `CIMUniqueValueRenderer`,
`CIMClassBreaksRenderer` (`SYM_AUTHORED`, line 6004) — and *reads but does not author* at least a
fourth (`CIMProportionalRenderer`, with an explicit "this form does not author it" message).
**Correction to the brief's framing:** the screen does not edit all nine CIM renderer families
today; it authors three, is explicit about a fourth it can only display, and anything else is the
raw-JSON door. That is a materially smaller authoring surface than "nine renderer families," and
worth knowing before redesigning for a scope the code doesn't actually claim.

**B. Populate the classes from data** (`Read the values` / `Read the data`). For the two
classifying rules, this is *always* a bulk, one-shot operation — there is no realistic path where
someone hand-builds 81 rows with **Add a class**. Today it is one more row in a long settings
list, visually identical in weight to `Draw:` and `Field:` above it, with nothing marking it as
"press this once and the shape of the rest of the page changes."

**C. Do something to the classes.** With one class, trivial. With many, this is a *different*
task — mostly "treat most of them the same way, hand-tune a few" — and today the screen only
offers "select exactly one, edit its stack precisely." See §2.

**D. Decide what applies to the whole renderer**, independent of how many classes exist: a
continuous ramp (`Vary with a number`), and — missing today — an overall fade. Currently rendered
as a third `<h4>` of identical weight to `Symbol sets` and `Symbol layers`, with nothing marking
it as renderer-scoped rather than per-class.

**E. Preview.** Correctly placed; caption is wrong after Store (finding 1).

**F. Save / discard / cancel.** Four buttons, two different scopes, no visual distinction (finding
5, §4).

**G. Reference, not a step: "What an ArcGIS client receives."** A compatibility diagnostic for
someone specifically asking "will this survive going to ArcGIS Pro," read by a minority of visits,
currently sitting in the main scroll of every visit and silently stale during editing (finding 1).

**Same object wearing two names:** *Symbol sets* (the gallery) and *Symbol layers* (the editable
stack) are the same thing — a class's symbol — shown twice, once as presets and once as the
current value. A person does not need both permanently visible; the gallery is a shortcut *into*
the stack, not a parallel place to be.

**What should be visible at once, every visit:** the rule, the field picker(s), the one class list
appropriate to the rule (bounded + filterable, already mostly right), the preview with a caption
that is never wrong, one save-state line, and two buttons (Store, Cancel).

**What should be behind something:**
- *Vary with a number* — collapsed by default behind something like "Add a data-driven ramp,"
  because it is occasional and renderer-wide, not because it is unimportant.
- *Symbol layers* (and *Symbol sets* folded into it as a tab) — becomes a drawer that opens **from
  the row it edits**, not a standing section below a 256-row list. This is not cosmetic: it
  structurally removes finding 4, because a panel that only exists while its own row's disclosure
  is open cannot ever be visible while that row is scrolled away — they are the same visual unit,
  not two that have to be kept in sync by hand.
- *What an ArcGIS client receives* — collapsed alongside the raw JSON document (`<details id=
  "symAdvanced">`, already correctly collapsed) rather than permanently rendered.

## 2. The many-class problem

Measured, not assumed: `ci_many` classified by its own `name` field alone produces **256**
classes — the exact ceiling the bounded-list code (`symBoxToTenRows`) was written against. Nobody
edits 81 or 256 rows one at a time as their primary path; they either accept the machine-generated
split near as-is and hand-tune a handful of standouts, or they want one rule applied to all of
them (a ramp, a fixed opacity, a symbol swap) with exceptions afterward. The current screen
answers only "select exactly one, edit precisely" — the ramp under *Vary with a number* exists,
but it's a **continuous** interpolation over a numeric field, bolted on as a fourth, seemingly
unrelated section, and is useless for a text-valued classification like this one (or the owner's
province names) by definition.

What ArcGIS Pro's classification pane and Map Viewer's category renderer both do, for the same
reason: separate *generating the breaks* from *batch-restyling the result* — a discrete colour
ramp applied across however many categorical classes exist (not the continuous two-stop kind this
screen already has), plus some form of acting on more than one row at once. I am not proposing
copying their chrome; the task need is real and this server already computes 90% of it — the
classify endpoint already assigns each of the 256 classes a distinct colour server-side (visible
in the "before Read the values" preview, a 16×16 swatch grid of genuinely different hues) — there
is simply no control to **reapply or adjust that palette afterward** without reclassifying from
scratch, and no way to act on more than one row at a time.

Concrete, minimal recommendation, scoped to what this codebase already has the pieces for:
- One control, above the class list, visible whenever there is more than one class: **"Recolour
  all from a ramp"**, reusing the palette machinery the classify endpoint already runs, plus
  **"Opacity, all classes"** (§3) — both act on the *whole* set regardless of scroll or filter
  position, answering the majority need without inventing a multi-select model.
- For the minority "hand-tune a few" need, the existing filter is already the right mechanism —
  live-confirmed it correctly narrows 256 rows to 1 on an exact match. The gap is only that
  leaving the filter loses your place (finding 4), which §1's per-row drawer removes structurally.
  Don't build a selection model to solve a problem the filter already solves; fix what's actually
  broken about it.
- The rows themselves render in **insertion order, not alphabetical or numeric order** (confirmed:
  `Parcel 0277, 0461, 0139, 0488…` — no discernible sort). For a list a person is expected to scan
  as well as search, an unsorted 256-row list makes scanning pointless and leans the whole design
  on the filter box working — which it does, but say so; sorting the list (by value, the field
  already on screen) costs nothing and makes "scroll near where I expect it" a second, valid way
  to navigate it, not just search.

## 3. Where opacity belongs

Two opacity concepts already exist on this page and are correctly placed; a third is missing, and
finding 7 is that missing one, not a bug in the other two:

- **Per-symbol-layer alpha**, inside one class's stack (`symalpha`, today's only control). Correct
  to leave exactly where it is — a fill and its stroke genuinely can carry different alpha in CIM,
  and D-214 (same codebase, same day) already reasoned this through explicitly: *"opacity is per
  symbol layer and a class may have several."* Not the defect.
- **A continuous, data-driven transparency ramp** (`Vary with a number → how solid it is`,
  `CIMTransparencyVisualVariable`). Also correctly renderer-wide by design; its only problem is
  presentation weight (§1), not placement.
- **Missing: a single renderer-wide opacity that scales every existing class's current alpha by
  the same factor** — "fade the whole layer," independent of which class happens to be selected,
  independent of whether a ramp exists. This is almost certainly what "alpha doesn't seem to do
  anything" meant in practice: with many similarly-hued classes, the only opacity box on the page
  changes one of them, and one province out of 81 changing shade is invisible at map scale — I
  measured the equivalent case directly: one class's fill alpha set to 20% changed 1 value in a
  256-class document, the other 255 untouched.

Add the missing one, next to the "recolour all" ramp control from §2, since both are "act on every
class" operations belonging in the same place. Implement it as a **multiply-through on Store**
(each class's own alpha × the chosen fraction), because CIM has no renderer-level opacity property
to target — this is a console-side batch edit over existing per-layer values, not a fourth kind of
opacity to reconcile with the other two later, and its label should say exactly that ("scales
every class's own opacity — a class already at 50% becomes 25%, not 100%") so nobody mistakes it
for a fourth persistent concept.

Do not relocate the existing per-layer alpha box. The fix is additive (give the renderer-wide case
a home it doesn't have) and structural (once the symbol-layer panel is a drawer attached to its
own row, per §1, it is visually unambiguous that the alpha box inside it belongs to "the row this
is open under," not to the layer as a whole).

## 4. The saving model

One model, and it is already a reasonable one, stated in the code's own comment: *"The document
stays, and it is the one thing that gets stored… what is in this box is what Store sends."* One
in-memory document; every control writes into it; nothing reaches the server until **Store**;
**Cancel** (top of page, session-wide) abandons the whole in-memory document across every tab
(Maintenance/Symbology/Caching/General), not just this one. No autosave, no per-section save. That
model does not need to change.

What needs to change is that five surfaces currently claim to report it and only two are ever
corrected (finding 1):

- **`#symState` (top line): keep as the single source of truth.** Already correct on load and
  after Store.
- **`#symPreviewState` (caption): fix the bug or delete the sentence.** It unconditionally writes
  "Not stored yet…" on every render and nothing ever corrects it after a real Store — a one-line
  fix (mirror `#symState`'s message after a successful Store), or, more robustly, remove the
  caption's own wording entirely and let the one state line above the two-column layout be the
  only sentence answering "is this saved," since a reader who already read it once does not need a
  second, independently-maintained copy of the same fact three inches away.
- **`#symClassifySays`: stop asserting a save-state that goes stale.** Its "Nothing is stored yet
  — press Store to keep them" is correct the moment it's written and wrong forever after, since
  nothing clears it on Store. Either clear it on a successful Store, or drop the save-state clause
  from it entirely (say only "256 classes from name" and point at the one state line for the
  rest).
- **Toast: no change**, correct and appropriately momentary.
- **"What an ArcGIS client receives": stop rendering it live-looking.** It is a snapshot of the
  *last successful Store*, not of the form above it, and during editing it visibly disagreed with
  the form in this session (2 classes shown while 256 sat unsaved above it) with no note that this
  is expected. Collapsing it behind the same disclosure as the raw document (§1) mostly solves
  this by removing it from the default view; if it stays visible, it needs one sentence saying
  what it actually reflects.

Net effect: one correct, always-visible line; everything else that used to compete with it is
either fixed to agree with it or removed from the default view.

## 5. What to remove — blunt

- **The preview caption's own wording.** Redundant with the state line even once the bug above is
  fixed; two sentences answering the same question is finding 1's shape, and there is no reason to
  keep it as two once one of them is no longer lying.
- **"Symbol sets" as a permanently-rendered gallery.** Fold into the per-class drawer (§1) as a
  tab next to "Layers." It edits the same object the layers panel edits; it does not need its own
  permanent scroll real estate on every visit, including the 90% of visits that never touch it.
- **"Symbol layers" as a permanently-rendered section below a long class list.** Same move, same
  reason — and it structurally removes finding 4 as a side effect, which a purely cosmetic fix
  would not.
- **"What an ArcGIS client receives," permanently visible.** Move behind the same disclosure as
  the raw JSON document. It's a compatibility check for someone deliberately asking whether this
  will survive going to ArcGIS Pro, not a shape decision every visit needs to see.
- **Nothing about "Vary with a number" itself should be removed** — its two-stop editor already
  correctly stays hidden until `Change:` is set to something (`symVaryRows`, already `hidden` by
  default). Only its *section-level prominence* — an `<h4>` the same size as Symbol Sets and
  Symbol Layers — should drop, since it is renderer-wide and occasional, not per-class.
- **"Add a class" should stay** — it is a genuine escape hatch (a manually-added catch-all class),
  not the way anyone builds a 256-row list — but it should stop sitting as a visual peer of "Read
  the values," which is the button that actually builds the list.
- **Do not remove Cancel, and do not treat it as a duplicate of the bottom three.** It is
  correctly scoped one level up (the whole multi-tab edit session, by deliberate design — "one
  Save and one Cancel for the session"). The actual defect is that nothing on screen signals that
  scope difference, so it visually reads as a fourth peer of Store/Fetch current/Back to
  generated when it is not one. Fix by labelling or grouping (e.g. keep it visually separated as
  it already is, but consider "Cancel — discard everything on every tab" as a tooltip/aria-label,
  since the plain word carries no scope information today), not by moving or removing it.
- **Regroup, don't remove, the bottom three.** They are not functional duplicates — *Fetch
  current* reloads from the server without leaving the page, *Back to generated* deletes the
  stored document (confirmed: it's a destructive call behind a native confirm dialog — *"Clear
  ci_many's symbology? It goes back to the generated appearance…"*), *Store* saves — but they are
  presented with identical ghost-button weight in a row that reads as three equally-safe choices.
  The destructive one should look different (e.g., no different position needed, but a distinct,
  less inviting treatment than "Fetch current," which is genuinely low-risk).

## Summary of concrete changes, roughly in effort order

1. Fix `#symPreviewState` to agree with `#symState` after Store (one line; the state-line's own
   bug, not a design question) — finding 1.
2. Stop `#symClassifySays` from asserting a save-state that goes stale after Store — finding 1.
3. Turn the permanently-rendered *Symbol sets* + *Symbol layers* sections into a drawer that opens
   from the class row it edits — removes finding 4 structurally, and is the biggest single
   simplification of the page's scroll length.
4. Add a renderer-wide *Opacity, all classes* control and a *Recolour all from a ramp* control,
   both above the class list — answers finding 7 and the actual many-class task (§2/§3).
5. Move *Vary with a number* and *What an ArcGIS client receives* behind disclosures; drop their
   section-level visual weight to match their occasional, non-per-class nature.
6. Filter the *Vary with a number → With:* field picker to numeric-typed fields, or at minimum
   warn when the selected field is not — finding 2.
7. Sort the class list by value; it is currently insertion-order, which makes scanning
   impossible and leans the whole design on the filter box without saying so.
8. Re-check finding 3 (line symbols on a polygon layer) against a layer whose geometry the current
   `symKindOfGeometry` detector might mis-read, since it did not reproduce here — five minutes, not
   a redesign item.
